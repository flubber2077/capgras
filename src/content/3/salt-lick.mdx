I went back and forth on love like cows coming and going\
to their corner of the paddock. I squatted in the chewed-down grass

and grew smaller under every tongue that found me. Wanting \
to eat the world and be consumed by it, I left my gated garden expecting

another, larger garden. Gums numb from gnawing the homeland’s heart-\
shaped leaves, I hoped to puff on ghost pipes from an older understory. 

Those anemic shoots of moon the signs of a season which never existed\
on my islands. Prelapsarian archipelago with annual autumns, sure, but no 

incarnadine <i>Fall</i>. At home, the gorse bloomed constantly to keep the kissing ripe\
and treacherous. One of my sweethearts gifted me a sprig. Intermittently 

forgotten in a pocket, it pricked teensy stigmata though my jeans. \
Clothes clung to me differently then. Everyone and the ocean 

knew the shape of my body. Reading all this, you could picture me\
unduly beautiful. It is important that you know I wasn’t terribly

pretty, but at the time you could tell I liked being alive. I made myself \
the winking margarita rim and then the surreptitious lick mid-sentence. 

I was both the crystal pillar and the one not turning to look back – walking\
past the ragged edge of the aurora, cracking like a seedpod on summer air.
